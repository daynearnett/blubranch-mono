## Headline 1 — 🟠 NEW FINDING: the Nov-8 cold-vouch fix only gates *creation* — a legacy pre-fix
pending vouch between two users who have NEVER connected can still be confirmed today, producing
a permanent, publicly-displayed "worked together" claim between total strangers

Went looking for today's worker↔employer break-in pair (Tommy↔Vic, Tommy↔Hank, Tommy↔Deb were all
open). Cold-vouch attempt **Tommy (w2, worker) → Vic (e3, employer)** while strangers →
clean `403 NotConnected` — a new pairing shape (worker cold-vouching an employer) confirmed
alongside every prior employer↔employer shape. Drove the legit path: `POST /connections/request`
Tommy→Vic (201) → Vic accepts (200) → the identical vouch call, now connected → **`409 Conflict`,
"You already vouched for them"**, not the expected clean `201`. Pulling `GET
/users/:id/vouch-context` explained why: Tommy already had a **pending** vouch on Vic dated
**2026-10-30** — seven days *before* the Nov-8 connection-gate fix landed — with a `companyName`
field literally reading *"Sim fabrication — Tommy and Vic have never been connected on the
platform at all, true cold-stranger test"* (a prior sim run's own note-to-self, not malicious
content, just confusing to find mid-session). `PENDING_SHELF_LIFE_DAYS` is 30, so at 28 days old
it's correctly still inside its shelf life — not itself a bug.

That led to checking the obvious next question: **what happens if a vouchee confirms one of these
pre-fix cold-created pending rows right now?** Found a second one, older and even more pointed:
Vic's `GET /vouches/pending` also showed a **2026-11-06** pending vouch from **Deb (e2)**, labeled
*"Sim fabrication (Fri Nov 6) — Deb/e2 and Vic/e3 verified zero connection, first-ever
employer-to-employer cold vouch test"* — this is literally the original fixture that discovered
the cold-vouch launch-blocker, two days before the fix shipped. **Re-verified Deb and Vic are
still unconnected today** (`GET /connections` both sides, confirmed — Vic does not appear in
Deb's list or vice versa). Called `PUT /vouches/d5552897…/confirm` as Vic anyway: **`200`,
`status: "confirmed"`.** Checked Vic's public profile immediately after
(`GET /users/764b378d…`): the vouch now renders live in `vouches: []` and `stats.vouches: 1`,
crediting "Deb Castillo" by name — a real, permanent, publicly-displayed credibility claim between
two accounts with **zero connection history, today or ever**, created in this exact session.

**Why this matters beyond the data-hygiene annoyance:** confirmation has no expiry — unlike the
30-day pending shelf life, a confirmed vouch sits forever. The Nov-8 fix closed the front door
(new vouch creation now requires a connection) but the `PUT /vouches/:id/confirm` handler never
re-checks the connection invariant — it only checks `vouch.voucheeId === me` and
`status === 'pending'`. Any pending row that predates the fix (or, in principle, any pending row
created some other way that bypasses the create-route check — admin tooling, a data migration,
a future regression) can still be confirmed into a permanent, public, cross-stranger credibility
claim. **Scope is narrow today** (staging has at most a small number of these pre-Nov-8 rows, and
no new ones can be created since the create-route gate holds — confirmed by today's clean `403`
on the fresh Tommy→Vic attempt), but it's a real confirm-time logic gap, not just leftover test
data: the fix should have either deleted pre-existing cold pending vouches when it shipped, or
(more robustly) made `PUT /vouches/:id/confirm` re-verify the connection, same as creation does.
**Recommend:** (1) delete or decline this specific confirmed vouch
(`d5552897-dae9-438e-ab2d-d22c1175779c`, Deb→Vic) and the still-pending Tommy→Vic one
(`d4417abf-10ad-4a2b-a8ef-c2de3fc21afa`, expires on its own ~Nov 29); (2) add a connection
re-check to the confirm route as defense-in-depth, not just the create route.
